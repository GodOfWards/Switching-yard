# FA yard operations: reference

How a Ferrocarriles Argentinos yard handled freight: who did the work, the
order trains were formed in, how switching was done, the paperwork, and
how a yard crew got the main line. Researched 2026-10-09 (#45) for jobs
stages 2 and 3 (#49, #50).

**Decided (Tom):**
- **Traffic sources** (2026-10-09): the era stays FA, 1977 or later.
  Product mix, seasons and destinations may come from modern data; the
  kinds of traffic and trains come from the FA sources here; volumes are
  labelled estimates. Modern block-train operation isn't carried back.

**Status:** the rules are **Confirmed**, read on FA's own rulebook, the
*Reglamento Interno Técnico Operativo* (RITO), in the text that carries
FA's amendments to May 1993, so it covers the game's era (1977 or later).
How crews actually organised the work comes from ex-staff accounts of two
other yards (**Secondary**). **Nothing specific to Junín's yard was
found**: its track roles, traffic and crews stay open.

## The sequence at a yard

Pieced together from the RITO; no single article lists it.

1. **Arrival and breaking up** (*desmembramiento*: taking a train apart
   to classify its traffic, Ferrosario). The guard reports defects noted
   on the way (art. 412).
2. **Inspection** by the car inspector (*revisor de vehículos*). A car
   with damage that could affect running is held until he sees it and
   doesn't move without his say (art. 414). A car he judges needs the
   shops is labelled on both sides for that shop; a loaded one that can
   still run is labelled *"Para talleres una vez vacío"* (for the shops
   once empty) (art. 417).
3. **Classification**: switching moves, planned in advance (art. 365 c).
4. **Formation** (*formación*: building a train from classified traffic
   or from breaking up another, Ferrosario) in the order art. 197 sets
   (below).
5. **Brake test.** Where there is a brake-test plant or a switcher with
   test gear, air-braked cars are tested *before* the road loco couples,
   so no switching is needed afterwards to pull out cars that fail
   (art. 450, amended Res. P. 888/75, 1975).
6. **Station checks** before the train is handed to the guard: coupled in
   formation order, hoses joined, no brakes on, cards on the cars, doors
   shut, seals in order on loaded cars, loads within gauge and sheeted,
   livestock standing, tail signals on the last vehicle (art. 207 a).
7. **Guard's checks**: walks the train, notes the loco number and the
   crew's names, tells the driver the time and **the train's weight, in
   axles or vehicles and tonnes**; anything not put right goes on the
   vehicle list (art. 207 d).
8. **Vehicle list** (*foja de ruta de vehículos*) made out and signed
   (art. 213), then departure with the block authority (below).

## Classification and train formation

| Fact | Source | Status |
|---|---|---|
| **A freight train's formation order**, from the loco back: (1) air-braked vehicles, as many as the brake table needs, *in order of destination* to the train's terminal, not dangerous goods; (2) locos hauled dead without brake; (3) livestock cars; (4) dangerous goods mid-train, at least 6 vehicles from the loco, with at least 3 cars of non-dangerous goods before and after; (5) the other cars **in the order the *Manual de Formación y Afectación de los Trenes* sets**, two-axle cars always at the rear; (6) damaged cars the inspector lets run; (7) hand-brake cars set by the appendix for each zone; (8) the van (*furgón*) | art. 197 a (amended 1976, 1977, 1982) | Confirmed |
| The *Manual de Formación y Afectación de los Trenes*, which held the real per-line block order, was **not found online** | searches 2026-10-09 | Unconfirmed: an FA or archive copy would settle it |
| **Intermediate stations**: the guard keeps the formation in mind so cars picked up go **next to cars for the same destination or junction**, unless the Control Office says otherwise. Station chiefs keep the day's loaded cars **together, in station order, clear of the fouling points**, not scattered among other loads or empties | art. 198 | Confirmed |
| Small cars, empties and two-axle broad-gauge cars go at the rear | art. 203 | Confirmed |
| Livestock, service cars and explosives: switch them as little as possible; where possible **set them on a track apart** while the rest is switched | art. 202 b, 382 | Confirmed |
| Protective empties may be added to separate dangerous goods, with the Control Office's consent, and swapped for loads on the way | art. 200 | Confirmed |
| **1970s: the *Plan de Encaminamiento de Vagones*** (wagon routing plan), "drawn up by the French", brought new train numbers, loco rotations and new forms for the yard, the freight shed and the freight office. It ordered cars by destination, **farthest intermediate stations first**, then the van. At Mar del Plata this was soon dropped because it delayed single-staffed stations | Mar del Plata Cargas | Secondary |
| The order Mar del Plata went back to: loco brakes (the braked cars), transshipment cars, traffic for the nearest stations (Tandil included), traffic for the Sarmiento, traffic for other railways, the van | Mar del Plata Cargas | Secondary |
| **Humps** (*lomo de asno*): no Argentine yard with one was found; an account of chalk-marked cars pushed over a hump is Spanish (Renfe), not FA | searches 2026-10-09 | Unconfirmed |

## How switching was done

| Fact | Source | Status |
|---|---|---|
| **Every move is directed by a switchman** (*cambista*) by hand signals; "cambista" covers the station chief, an assistant, a guard or anyone acting as one. A light engine with no switchman may move on a fixed signal or the signalman's hand signals | art. 365 a, b | Confirmed |
| **Switching is planned in advance** so it is done correctly and quickly, and everyone cooperates to do it **in the least time**. The station chief supervises in person when he can | art. 365 c, d | Confirmed |
| The switchman **tells the driver the moves before starting** | art. 366 | Confirmed |
| Before a move: people warned, switchmen out from between cars, cars fit to move (doors, loads), tracks clear, **hand brakes off and chocks out**, derails closed | art. 367 | Confirmed |
| The driver doesn't move, or pass any switch or derail, without the switchman's hand signal (and the fixed signal, where there is one). The switchman checks the points are set and closed first | art. 368–370 | Confirmed |
| A switch behind a moving cut is thrown only once its last axle is clear; one ahead only if there is time before the first axle reaches it | art. 372 | Confirmed |
| **Kicking** (*largada*: the loco pushes uncoupled cars to send them on alone) is allowed, with the least speed that will do, judged from track, weight of the cut, wind and distance. A second kick waits until the first cut has cleared the switch | art. 384, 385 b | Confirmed |
| **Kicking is forbidden** with or against: passenger stock, **tank cars**, loads that can shift, cars with people in them, **livestock or explosives**, **cars without working brakes**; and with wind or grade that could run away, poor visibility, or toward an open level crossing | art. 385 a | Confirmed |
| **Flying switches are forbidden** (uncoupling on the move and throwing the switch between loco and cars). Rope switching only in force majeure | art. 386, 387 | Confirmed |
| Rough switching is to be avoided: no damage to the cars or their loads | art. 389 | Confirmed |
| On a grade, switch with the loco on the low end, or a braked vehicle there with a man on it | art. 378 | Confirmed |
| **When switching ends**: cars secured so they can't move, fouling points clear, derails back to normal (art. 392 c). Parked cars are left **clear of the fouling points**, protected by derails, braked, and coupled to each other; the end car of a track leading to the main line is chocked | art. 392 c, 393 | Confirmed |
| A loco may run at either end of what it moves during switching in stations and to their yards; otherwise the lead loco runs at the head | art. 236 b | Confirmed |
| A loco crosses a car weighbridge only if unavoidable, with the scale locked, at **5 km/h** at most | art. 394 | Confirmed |
| Coupling: four-axle cars with the screw coupler screwed fully tight; two-axle cars with their link chain | art. 210 | Confirmed |

## Who did what

| Role | Work | Source | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Cambista** (switchman) | Directs moves, throws hand switches, couples and uncouples | art. 365; La Paternal | Confirmed |
| **Capataz cambista** (switching foreman) | Leads the switching crew | La Paternal; Mar del Plata | Secondary |
| **Auxiliar de playa** (yard assistant) | **Orders the day's work** in the yard | La Paternal | Secondary |
| **Señalero** (signalman) | Works the cabin; one per shift at La Paternal | art. 371; La Paternal | Confirmed |
| Switcher crew | A driver (*conductor*) and assistant (*ayudante*) on the yard engine, called the "Pilota" at La Paternal | La Paternal | Secondary |
| **Revisor de vehículos** (car inspector) | Inspects cars in train formation, light repairs, axle-box lubrication, labels bad-order cars | art. 195 c, 414, 417; MR 4-002 art. 4 | Confirmed |
| Encargado de cargas (freight clerk) | Keeps the car register, dispatches loaded and empty cars, **fills in the waybills** (*cartas de porte*), works the weighbridge | La Paternal | Secondary |
| Controladores (tally clerks) | Check door seals, log cars on a form | La Paternal; Mar del Plata | Secondary |
| **Jefe de estación** | Supervises switching; no one outside the station moves cars without his consent | art. 365 d, 391 | Confirmed |
| **Oficina de Control** (the control office, the dispatcher) | Orders and authorises: adding cars to passenger and mixed trains, protective empties, special trains, receives reports of cars held | art. 195, 200, 415 | Confirmed |
| **Guarda** (guard, the train's chief) | Rides the van; checks the train, keeps the vehicle list, helps with switching at intermediate stations | art. 206, 207, 213, 512 | Confirmed |

**Crew sizes, Mar del Plata Cargas, 1964** (Secondary), totals across
both shifts: 3 first-class and 1 third-class assistants, 3 tally clerks,
2 switching foremen, 7 switchmen, 2 labourers, 2 signalmen; in the 1970s
one assistant, one foreman and one switchman were cut. Car department: a
supervisor and 3 inspectors. At its end it ran with 1 foreman and 2
switchmen.

**Repair levels** (MR 4-002 art. 4, Confirmed): an *estación revisora*
inspects cars in formation and does minimal repairs; a *desvío* does
light repairs, including lifting a car to change wheelsets or a bogie; a
*taller* (shops) does the programmed A, B, C and D repairs.

## Paperwork

| Fact | Source | Status |
|---|---|---|
| **Foja de ruta de vehículos** (vehicle list): made out by the train's starting station, **listing the vehicles by number with every column filled in**, and anomalies seen in loads or vehicles. Signed by the guard and the station employee. Intermediate stations add the cars they attach, and sign receipt for those left | art. 213 | Confirmed |
| Its columns | not found | Unconfirmed: a blank form or a photo would confirm |
| **Train weight** = each vehicle's tare plus its load in tonnes, summed. The tare is stencilled on the car's side. Fractions under 500 kg are dropped, those over count as a tonne | art. 211 a, b | Confirmed |
| A car weighed on the way takes the weight on the latest weighing slip (*boleta de verificación*) attached to its **waybill** (*guía oficial*) | art. 211 c | Confirmed |
| Fixed weights: lot cars with goods to deliver, tare + 7 t; freight vans, tare only; livestock by head (export steer 500 kg, common cattle 400, horse 350, large calf 150, pig 100, sheep or goat 50) | art. 211 d | Confirmed |
| A train may not exceed the tonnage table for its loco class | art. 212 | Confirmed |
| Each car carries its **card** (*tarjeta*) | art. 207 a 3 | Confirmed (what the card shows was not found) |
| Dangerous goods: a red "Peligro" label on both sides, and the same word on the waybills and envelopes (*guías y sobres*) | art. 201 | Confirmed |
| Waybills (*cartas de porte*) filled in by the freight clerk; freight work recorded in "Book 404" | La Paternal | Secondary |
| The yard crew's own switch list: **no FA name or form was found**. The RITO only says switching is planned beforehand and told to the driver | — | Unconfirmed |

## Authority to move

| Fact | Source | Status |
|---|---|---|
| **Running tracks vs sidings**: *vías de corrida* are the main and the loops trains run, cross and pass on; *desvíos* are tracks for switching and storing vehicles. An *estación cabecera* is any station that starts, forms or re-forms trains | art. 26 | Confirmed |
| A **switching signal** (letter "M" on colour lights) never authorises going on to the next block station; clear, it allows as far as the move needs, stopping short of obstructions, hand switches not shown clear, and absolute signals at danger | art. 116 | Confirmed |
| **Double track: to switch out beyond the home signal** into the block section, the station asks the block station behind for permission with bell code No. 3, only if the instruments show the section clear | art. 44 | Confirmed |
| While a train is in the section ahead, the station it left **may occupy that section with switching** | art. 61 a 2 | Confirmed |
| Single track: no train enters a block section without a departure order (staff or ticket), **except when switching** | art. 49 | Confirmed |
| A station whose tracks are obstructed by switching may still accept a train with a **conditional line clear**; the train is stopped at the station behind and told in writing it must stop at the home signal | art. 64 | Confirmed |
| **Stopping the switching for a train**: the station or signalman waves a red flag or light up and down. The switchman stops at once, clears the switches, sets hand switches to normal, and acknowledges with the same signal; nothing moves on them until he has | art. 392 a, b | Confirmed |
| Switching beyond station limits: pull out, push back; otherwise make sure no car was left outside (a man rides the far car, or the far car's number or the count is checked) | art. 379 | Confirmed |
| Train classes asked for by bell code include **through freight** (*tren de carga directo*, 2-4), **pick-up freight** (*tren de carga de maniobras*, 2-5), livestock (2-3), and parcels, milk, fruit or fish (2-2) | art. 42 | Confirmed |
