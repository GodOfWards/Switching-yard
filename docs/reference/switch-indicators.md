# Switch indicators: how Argentine railways show a switch's position

Researched 2026-10-08.

## Decided (Tom)

- **2026-10-08:** switches show a green disc when set normal and a yellow
  triangle on black when reversed, after RGF art. 100. Zoomed out, track is
  a single line; zoomed in, rails and ties. The leg a switch isn't set to
  fades out near the switch.

## Argentina: Reglamento General de Ferrocarriles, art. 100

The national General Railway Regulation (Decreto-Ley 8.302/1957, 1960
edition, supplement updated to 31/12/1995) covers the discs and indicators
beside switches (p. S.40). It was in force through the FA era.
Source: [suplemento_rgf.pdf](https://www.argentina.gob.ar/sites/default/files/suplemento_rgf.pdf).

| Indicator | Shows | Meaning | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Switch indicator (*indicador de cambio*) | Green disc or rectangle | Set for the main track | Confirmed |
| Switch indicator | Yellow triangle on black | Set to diverge | Confirmed |
| Position indicator (*indicador de posición de cambio*), Decreto 6534/1946 | White vertical bar, 70 × 200 mm, on black | Set for the straight or less-diverging track | Confirmed |
| Position indicator | White horizontal arrow on black, point toward the diverging side | Set for the more-diverging track on that side | Confirmed |

At night the position indicator is milky-white glass lit by a white lamp,
or white enamel with reflectors (same article). The art. 100 discs show
green when clear and red when blocked. These are protection discs, not
switch targets.

Not found: whether FA yards actually fitted these indicators to hand-thrown
yard switches, or which of the two kinds they used. A photo of an FA-era
yard switch stand, or the RITO's text on *cambios*, would confirm it.

## Elsewhere, for contrast (Secondary)

North American railways had no single standard. In yards, green or white
usually meant normal and yellow or red meant reversed. Red/green was mostly
kept for main-track switches. Sources: forum discussions on
[trains.com](https://forum.trains.com/t/freight-yard-switch-signals/192200)
and [RYPN](https://www.rypn.org/forums/viewtopic.php?p=268637).
