# The *Aconcagua* under FA: reference

Researched 2026-11-23 for #77. Status words follow [README.md](README.md).

**Decided (Tom, 2026-11-24, #77):**
- **Build it on these facts**, with the gaps marked Assumed.
- **Consist:** an RSD-16 and 7 coaches, in this order: F (van), CT ×2,
  P ×2, R, PA. Retunable.
- **One Materfer body**, a vehicle kind for each FA code, labelled by its
  code. Livery from the Mendoza photo below.
- **Length:** 25 m a coach. **Assumed** (no source; European coaches of
  the era run ~24–26 m over buffers); a Materfer diagram would replace it.
  Retunable.
- **The RSD-16 is a second loco type** ([locomotives.md](locomotives.md)).
- **Timetable:** 565 from Retiro reaches the map's east edge at 15:30;
  566 from Mendoza its west edge at 23:30. **Assumed** (no FA timetable
  found); picked so every shift sees two through trains. Retunable.
- **At night a passenger train gets no conditional line clear** (#104;
  [fa-yard-operations.md](fa-yard-operations.md), Authority to move).

**What the game assumes beyond the decisions** (implementation, retunable):
a coach's tare is 45 t (**Assumed**, analogy with European coaches of the
same length; it only matters if something runs into the train), and its
bearings are roller (**Unconfirmed**).

**Bottom line.** The train's identity, route, class mix and usual locomotive are
findable. Coach lengths, the coach livery, and any timetable through Junín were
**not found** on the open web. Those need a scanned FA *itinerario* / *guía de
horarios* or a dated photo set (see "What would close the gaps").

## Consist

Two sources, which don't fully agree:

- **es.wikipedia FA table** lists the Aconcagua's accommodation codes as
  **DA, PA, P, CT, R, X, F**: air-conditioned sleeper, pullman, first,
  tourist, restaurant, car-carrying flat, guard/baggage van. *Secondary* (read
  through a summary of the page, not verbatim; re-read before relying on it).
- **Diario Democracia (2021)** describes the "social" trains (Zonda,
  Sanjuanino, Aconcagua) as: loco, parcels van, mail van, first and second
  class, "no había pullman"; "dos coches de la primera y segunda clase y se
  completaba con un vagón de clase Turista y el comedor". It adds the
  Aconcagua was "con mejores prestaciones" than the other two. *Secondary (oral).*

Likely reading: the table is the 1980s Aconcagua (after it absorbed the
Zonda's slot and gained pullman/sleepers); the newspaper describes the older
*Zonda*-type social train. **Unconfirmed.**

**Coach count: not found.** For scale only, a comparable 2010 FA-heritage
long-distance train (Tren Patagónico, forotrenes) ran: car flat ×2, cinema,
pullman ×2, sleeper ×3, dining, tourist ×3, generator van, guard van (14
vehicles). A plausible Aconcagua would be ~10–14 vehicles: **Assumed**, rests
on that analogy and on "~700 t" trailing load a driver gave for the Cuyo
expresses (Diario Democracia). A dated photo or *formación* sheet would replace it.

  diagram (*plano*) would confirm. If the game needs a number now, treat it

  - Coaches: **light grey body, a thin red stripe at the bottom of the

    coaches further back read as the same grey. Sampled colours: body

  - RSD-16 8500: FA scheme of **yellow-orange body, red lower band, dark
    blue-grey roof, black and yellow chevrons on the pilot**.
  - Status: **Secondary** (read off one photo, colour film, one vehicle).
    More dated photos would confirm it held through the 1980s.

## Locomotive

- **ALCo RSD-16** was the everyday power. RailPictures caption: RSD-16s
  **8500 and 8454** entering Retiro with 566 (undated). Commons photo of the
  train at Mendoza: RSD-16 **8500**. Los Andes: RSD-16 **8488** booked for the
  last *Cuyano*, 1993. A forum notes "la ALCo RSD 16 era lo más cotidiano" on
  these trains. *Secondary.*
- The San Martín's RSD-16s were delivered 1957–59 as 4301–4345; the 8xxx
  numbers above imply a later FA renumbering. *Unconfirmed* (no source states it).
- A Junín-based driver recalls **GM GT22CW** ("GT2 CW, 2.400 HP", 100–120 km/h,
  ~700 t) on the Cuyo expresses; a GR12W is said to have taken the Aconcagua
  once. *Secondary (oral);* GT22s may have been the *Libertador*'s power more than the Aconcagua's.
- Its specs are in [locomotives.md](locomotives.md).

## Timetable through Junín

**Not found.** No FA timetable for 565/566 turned up online. What exists:
journey "16 h minimum" Buenos Aires–Mendoza; train 512 *Cuyano* booked out of
Mendoza at 16:30 in 1993; a San Juan departure "a las 13" on the last day.
None of these give a Junín time. Any Junín time in the game is **Assumed**
until an *itinerario* is found. Today's Retiro–Junín train (563/564, 2026:
Retiro 18:15 → Junín ~00:20; Junín 01:10 → Retiro ~07:28) reuses the old
number block but is a different train.

## What would close the gaps

1. An FA *Guía de horarios* / *itinerario* for the San Martín, any year
   1978–1992 (Junín times, consist codes). Likely holders: Museo Ferroviario
   (Retiro), Biblioteca Nacional, Archivo Histórico de Junín (Sagastume's
   notes cite one), ForoTrenes / Portal de Trenes members.
2. Dated photos: Flickr user milandimitri's set (source of "8500 Mendoza"),
   RailPictures Argentina, *Todo Trenes* magazine.
3. Materfer coach diagrams for length and weight (leivag1991 blog draws them
   but prints no dimensions; Materfer catalogues).
